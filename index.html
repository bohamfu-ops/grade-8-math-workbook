Chapter 01 / Geometry

The Pythagorean theorem

Image 1 · Bell ringer · Grade 8, Week 2

Identify the hypotenuse, test side lengths, and find a rectangle’s diagonal.

Learn the math

Find the 90° corner

The legs meet at the right angle. The hypotenuse is across from that angle and is always the longest side. In the diagram, a and b are the legs and c is the hypotenuse.

Find a missing length

For a right triangle, a² + b² = c². To find the hypotenuse, add the squares of the legs and take the positive square root. To find a leg, subtract the square of the known leg from c² first.

Work backward

To test three possible side lengths, use the largest as c. If the two smaller squares add to c², the Pythagorean converse proves that the triangle is right.

Worked example

A rectangle measures 12 ft by 16 ft. Its edges meet at 90°, so the diagonal is √(12² + 16²) ft = √400 ft = 20 ft.

Geometry vocabulary & views

Image 2 · Week 5 Assessment · Questions 1–10

Learn the math

Face, net, and congruent

A face is one flat surface of a solid. A net is a flat arrangement of faces that folds into a solid. Congruent figures have exactly the same size and shape.

Set the viewing direction

Here, length runs left to right across the front, depth runs front to back, and height runs bottom to top. A straight-on view shows two of these directions.

Choose two dimensions

Top uses length × depth. Front uses length × height. Side uses depth × height. The missing dimension points along the viewing direction.

Worked example

For a box 9 in. long, 2 in. deep, and 4 in. high, the front view measures 9 in. × 4 in. Depth runs away from the viewer.
